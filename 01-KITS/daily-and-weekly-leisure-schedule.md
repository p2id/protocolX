# Daily and weekly leisure schedule: prompt and template

**Leisure is a biological and psychological necessity.** It can support mood regulation, attention restoration, and mental balance.

This standalone, editable template works on paper or in any text editor or text-based assistant; no app, account, or repository is required. It organizes timing and review, not activity content. The participant chooses what leisure means for them; do not prescribe, rank, recommend, or prefer any activity.

## Schedule-organizing instructions

Organize participant-chosen leisure into a daily or weekly plan. Exclude work, study, chores, errands, and obligations disguised as leisure.

1. Use exactly two session types with these exact labels: **real-world** and **digital**. Real-world uses the physical world as its main medium; digital uses a screen, device, or internet. Split a session if its medium changes. Each session has exactly one type.
2. Real-world is the default. Digital leisure is not inherently harmful; the cap is a neutral planning constraint, not a judgment.
3. Organize session count, timing, and duration only. Keep the activity field optional and participant-controlled. Where practical, keep sessions self-sufficient and feasible with resources already available; do not assume spending, subscriptions, special equipment, or another person's participation.
4. Adapt the structure to time, energy, access, safety, and context without publishing sensitive details.
5. Every digital session needs a short reason and an offline/real-world alternative. Leave these fields blank for real-world sessions.
6. Every session must have a post-session evaluation with exactly these three prompts: “What did it take?”, “What did it give?”, and “Would you repeat it?” The answer to the last prompt must be yes / no / with conditions. Do not add extra post-session evaluation prompts.
7. Include a weekly review of mood/restoration, digital-cap compliance, and possible offline/real-world alternatives.
8. Keep personal constraints and reflection answers private. Public copies exclude identifying or sensitive details and private reflections.

## Weekly digital-cap calculation

Calculate the cap separately for each seven-day week. For a one-day plan, calculate against that day's planned leisure total and apply the same percentage ceiling; for a plan spanning multiple weeks, show a separate calculation for each week. Never combine multiple weeks into one calculation to hide an over-cap week.

- Total planned leisure minutes (T) = minutes for **all** planned leisure sessions in that week, both types.
- Planned digital minutes (D) = minutes for **all** digital sessions in that same week.
- Digital share = planned digital minutes ÷ total planned leisure minutes × 100 = D ÷ T × 100.
- 20% ceiling = floor(T × 0.20) minutes.
- Applicable cap = the lowest of the 20% ceiling and any lower participant/configured limit or stricter applicable protocol limit.
- Show T, D, the formula with substituted numbers, the percentage, and the applicable cap. Confirm D is within the applicable cap and the digital share is no more than 20%.
- If a plan exceeds the cap, revise it before presenting it. Count every planned session consistently; do not omit sessions from T or D.
- If T is zero, the share is undefined; do not plan digital minutes until T is positive.

**Arithmetic illustration only:** if T = 120 and D = 10 minutes, then 10 ÷ 120 × 100 = 8.3%. The 20% ceiling is floor(120 × 0.20) = 24 minutes. If no stricter limit applies, the applicable cap is 24 minutes; 10 ≤ 24 and 8.3% ≤ 20%, so the plan is within the cap.

## Propose → Review → Approve → Publish

Prepare an anonymized, editable Markdown draft in the configured proposal workflow. Review leisure-only scope, participant choice, exact session types, digital reasons and alternatives, visible weekly cap arithmetic, all three evaluation prompts for every session, weekly review, privacy, portability, and standalone usability. An authorized human maintainer must explicitly approve the exact proposed version. Opening a PR, passing checks, or an automation run is not approval. Publish only after verified approval; never auto-publish or write an unapproved draft into the canonical community collection.
