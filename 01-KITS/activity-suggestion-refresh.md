# Activity suggestion refresh process

## Purpose

The leisure generator must not depend on an unchanging, prescriptive list of activities. It uses editable, non-branded activity-building patterns (actions, settings, sensory attention, gentle movement, imagination, and making with materials already available). These patterns are starting points, not a definition of the method.

## Refresh cadence

Review the activity-building patterns at least once every quarter, and sooner when a user reports an accessibility issue, a harmful assumption, or a stale/repetitive output. This is a documented maintainer process, not an automatic claim that research has already been checked.

## Research sources and method

Use public, general, preferably open-access sources such as:
- Systematic reviews and open-access papers about leisure, recreation, nature contact, play, social connection, rest, mood, and attention.
- Public-health and university research summaries that distinguish evidence from opinion.
- Open educational resources and public-domain cultural or community practices.
- Feedback from community schedule proposals, after removing identifying and sensitive details.

Search broadly across populations and contexts. Do not infer that one activity benefits everyone or treat correlational evidence as proof of causation. Do not scrape private communities. Do not use branded entertainment catalogues, commercial promotions, or paywalled material as the generator's default dependency.

## Public starting points for refreshes

These are starting points for future reviewers, not endorsements of a single activity or proof that all leisure has one effect:

- Heckel et al. (2024), *A systematic review of the wellbeing benefits of being active through leisure and fitness centres*, open access: https://doi.org/10.1080/02614367.2023.2243654
- Coventry et al. (2021), *Nature-based outdoor activities for mental and physical health: Systematic review and meta-analysis*, open access: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/8498096/
- Ohly et al. (2016), *Attention Restoration Theory: A systematic review of the attention restoration potential of exposure to natural environments*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27668460/
- Open-access search for leisure and wellbeing literature: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=leisure+wellbeing+review
- Open-access search for attention restoration literature: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=attention+restoration+systematic+review

These sources cover particular contexts and populations, not a universal prescription. The attention-restoration review itself notes limitations and uncertainty; preserve those qualifications when turning evidence into general activity patterns.

## Refresh workflow

1. **Collect:** record the public source title, author or issuing body, publication date, stable URL, access date, and a short description of the general finding. Do not copy long passages.
2. **Translate to principles:** identify broad, adaptable patterns (for example, optional gentle movement, unstructured rest, sensory observation, low-cost making, playful exploration, or chosen social connection). Avoid prescribing a named activity or assuming equipment, spending, location, physical ability, or company.
3. **Filter:** every candidate must be leisure-only, self-sufficient, immediately startable or require only resources already available, free/low-cost, adaptable, non-branded, and safe to decline. Real-world remains the default; digital is not inherently bad.
4. **Diversify:** vary sensory focus, activity shape, setting, intensity, social/solitary choice, and session duration. Remove near-duplicates and avoid optimizing for productivity or achievement.
5. **Review:** a second maintainer or community reviewer checks source quality, accessibility, overclaiming, and consistency with core rules.
6. **Update:** edit the generator's compositional patterns and this document; note the review date, sources, and changes in the commit message or a dated entry below. Preserve the portable prompt so it can be used without the app.
7. **Verify:** manually test several combinations of energy, constraints, output formats, schedule lengths, and digital caps. Confirm no branded entities, no non-leisure obligations, and no unexplained digital sessions.

## Research integrity

This process supports idea diversity; it does not provide medical advice or promise health outcomes. The primary framing is a design principle: leisure is a human biological and psychological necessity supporting mood regulation, attention restoration, and mental balance. Individual experience and accessibility needs take precedence over generic suggestions.
