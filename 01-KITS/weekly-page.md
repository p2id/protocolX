# Weekly page — leisure schedule and core review

Print or copy one page per seven-day week. For a plan spanning multiple weeks, use a separate page and calculation for each week. Leisure is a biological and psychological necessity; this page organizes participant-chosen leisure and restoration, not productivity. It does not prescribe, rank, or prefer any activity.

## Planned weekly digital-cap check

- Total planned leisure minutes (T), including real-world and digital sessions: ______
- Planned digital minutes (D): ______
- Digital share = planned digital minutes ÷ total planned leisure minutes × 100 = D ÷ T × 100 = ______ ÷ ______ × 100 = ______%
- 20% ceiling = floor(T × 0.20) = ______ minutes
- Applicable cap = the lowest of the 20% ceiling, the core protocol's 480-minute weekly maximum, and any lower participant/configured/applicable limit = ______ minutes
- D ≤ applicable cap? Yes / No
- Digital share ≤ 20%? Yes / No
- If T is zero, the share is undefined and the week cannot pass validation. Add planned leisure minutes before use; do not plan digital minutes while T is zero.
- If either check fails, revise the plan and recalculate before use or sharing.

The 20% ceiling is a neutral planning constraint, not a judgment that digital leisure is inherently harmful. The stricter applicable limit always governs.

## Privacy, portability, and publication

This page is editable, printable, and usable as standalone text/Markdown without an app, account, repository, or network. Keep identifying details, sensitive information, and private reflection answers out of public copies. No personal reflection is required for publication.

**Propose → Review → Approve → Publish:** submit an anonymized draft through the configured repository proposal workflow; review scope, exact session types, per-week arithmetic, digital reasons and alternatives, the three exact prompts, weekly review, privacy, and standalone usability; require explicit approval by an authorized human maintainer of the exact proposed version; publish only after that approval is verified. An open PR, passing checks, or an automation run is not approval. Never publish an unapproved draft.
