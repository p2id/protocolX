Leisure schedule generator

Leisure is a biological and psychological necessity, not a luxury. Its primary functions include mood regulation, attention restoration, and mental balance. This generator plans leisure for those functions—not for productivity.

1. Configure the schedule

Avoid names, exact locations, or sensitive details. This field is not included in public output.

2. Base rules and digital limits

The base rules come first: leisure only; exactly two session types; real-world leisure is the default; every digital session has a reason; and each session is reviewed by what it took, what it gave, and whether to repeat. The optional 20% ceiling is an extra planning constraint—not a replacement for the core rules, any applicable core-protocol limits, scoring, or calculations.

Every digital session needs a reason.

3. Edit every session

The generated schedule is a draft. Edit it freely. Real-world means the physical world is the main medium; digital means a screen, device, or internet is the main medium. If an experience changes medium, split it into separately timed sessions. Mixed is not a third type.

4. After-session evaluation

Keep these reflections private by default; they are never included in the public export.

6. Copy, download, and propose

Public exports include the schedule and rules, but exclude personal constraints, private reviews, and reflection fields. Review the result before sharing.

Community flow: propose → maintainer review → approve → publish. A proposal is not automatically added to the canonical collection. Repository for updates: github.com/p2id/protocolX.

Method and portability

Suggestions are composed from configurable activity shapes, settings, sensory attention, playful movement, observation, and making with materials already available—not selected from a fixed branded catalogue. Prefer activities that can start now, are free or low-cost, and do not depend on subscriptions, pre-produced content, purchases, specialized equipment, or another person's participation. Skip anything unsuitable for your access needs or context.

The activity-building rules are portable and editable. The tool works locally in your browser; it does not save or send your inputs to a server. To propose a public schedule, only the exported schedule is placed in a draft for you to review and submit.

Reuse and adapt. Rest, unstructured time, and an empty slot are valid. Missed sessions are information, not failure. Digital leisure is not inherently bad.
